# Document 1: Vision & Mission Document

## Purpose

Define, in language precise enough to survive a decade of decisions, why this company exists (mission), where it is going (vision), and what world it believes will exist if it succeeds. This is the root document — every other document in Phase 1 and beyond must trace back to it without contradiction.

## Why It Exists

Startups drift. Without a written, specific mission, "AI Life Operating System" quietly becomes "another to-do app with AI features" the first time a roadmap decision is made under deadline pressure. This document exists to be the tiebreaker: when two feature ideas compete for the same quarter, this document says which one is closer to the mission. It is also the document investors, early employees, and press will quote back to the company for years — it must be written to be quoted.

## Approximate Page Count

4–6 pages.

## Sections

1. **One-Sentence Mission** — "Build an AI that proactively manages a user's life instead of waiting for commands," plus 2–3 paragraphs unpacking what "proactive" and "manages" precisely mean (and don't mean — e.g., not autonomy without consent).
2. **10-Year Vision Narrative** — a concrete, sensory description of a user's day-to-day life in Year 10 if the company fully succeeds. Written as narrative, not bullet points.
3. **Why Now** — the technological, behavioral, and market shifts (LLM capability, ambient sensing, on-device inference cost curves, smartphone ubiquity) that make this achievable now and not five years ago.
4. **Category Claim** — the assertion that "AI Life Operating System" is a new category, distinct from "productivity app," "finance app," "health app," or "chatbot." Defines the boundary of the claim.
5. **What Success Looks Like at Scale** — qualitative description of the company at tens of millions of users (not metrics — those live in the Success Metrics Document — but what kind of company it has become).
6. **What We Will Never Become** — explicit anti-goals (e.g., "we will never sell raw personal data," "we will never optimize for time-on-app over user outcomes"). Anti-goals are as load-bearing as goals.
7. **Founding Story / Origin Rationale** — why this team, why this problem, briefly.

## Deliverables

* Approved Vision & Mission markdown/PDF document.
* A single canonical one-sentence mission string (used verbatim in pitch decks, website, onboarding, job postings — one source of truth, no paraphrased variants in the wild).
* A "Why Now" slide extractable for fundraising decks.

## Dependencies

None upstream. This is the root document of the entire documentation tree.

## Which Teams Use This

Founders/CEO (authorship), Product (roadmap tiebreaking), Marketing/Brand (external narrative), Fundraising/IR (pitch decks), People/Hiring (job descriptions, candidate pitch), Design (tone of voice foundation).

## Completion Criteria

* [ ] Mission statement is a single sentence, memorable without notes, and distinguishes the company from "productivity app," "finance app," and "health app" categories individually.
* [ ] Vision narrative is concrete enough that two different readers describe the same Year-10 world back, not two different products.
* [ ] Anti-goals section reviewed and explicitly approved by founders — not left as an afterthought.
* [ ] Signed off by: CEO/Founder(s) (required), Head of Product (required once hired).
