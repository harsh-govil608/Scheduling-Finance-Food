# Document 6: Success Metrics Document

## Purpose

Define the single North Star metric and the tree of supporting KPIs and guardrail metrics that will determine, unambiguously, whether the company is achieving its mission — as opposed to merely growing.

## Why It Exists

A company whose mission is "reduce manual effort" can easily and accidentally optimize for the opposite (engagement, session time, notification opens) if metrics aren't defined deliberately up front. This document exists to make sure the metrics the company actually manages toward are the mission's metrics, not generic app-growth metrics borrowed from a different kind of product. It is also the arbiter for the "never overwhelm" philosophy — guardrail metrics here are what stop growth-at-all-costs decisions later.

## Approximate Page Count

6–8 pages.

## Sections

1. **North Star Metric** — a single metric capturing "the AI is proactively managing the user's life, effectively," with explicit definition, formula, and why it was chosen over alternatives (e.g., not DAU/MAU alone, since that rewards reactive re-engagement, not reduced effort).
2. **Supporting KPI Tree** — metrics feeding the North Star, organized per pillar:
   * Productivity: e.g., % of schedule changes handled without manual reschedule, reminder-to-completion rate.
   * Finance: e.g., % of transactions auto-captured without manual entry, budget-prediction accuracy.
   * Health: e.g., % of meals logged via photo/voice vs. manual, streak-free adherence rate.
3. **Manual Effort Index** — a composite metric directly operationalizing "manual work should approach zero" (e.g., user-actions-required per week, trending down per user over their lifecycle), with target trajectory.
4. **Guardrail / Anti-Metrics** — metrics that must NOT be optimized upward at the expense of the mission (e.g., notification count, time-in-app as a vanity metric, "engagement" divorced from outcome), each paired with a ceiling or a rule ("time-in-app is not a success metric and must not appear in OKRs").
5. **Trust & Retention Metrics** — long-term signals (e.g., autonomy-level acceptance rate on the Proactivity Ladder, retention at 90/365 days) that indicate the AI is being trusted with more responsibility over time, not just used.
6. **Metric Ownership & Cadence** — who owns which metric, how often reviewed (weekly/monthly/quarterly), and which metrics gate major releases.
7. **Metric Evolution Plan** — acknowledgment that early metrics (pre-launch, low data) will be proxies, with a plan for graduating to real behavioral metrics post-launch.

## Deliverables

* Approved Success Metrics document.
* North Star Metric definition card (derived artifact) circulated company-wide.
* KPI tree diagram mapping every supporting metric to the North Star.
* Initial OKR template seeded from this document for use once Phase 2/3 planning begins.

## Dependencies

Requires **User Personas Document** (metrics must reflect real persona pain points), **Problem Statement Document** (metrics must measure the problem actually being solved), and **Product Philosophy Document** (guardrail metrics must operationalize "never overwhelm").

## Which Teams Use This

Product & Data leadership (primary owners), Executive/Founders (OKRs, board reporting), Engineering (instrumentation requirements feed Phase 4/5 architecture), Design (guardrail metrics inform notification/UX limits), Growth/Marketing (must plan campaigns that don't conflict with anti-metrics).

## Completion Criteria

* [ ] North Star metric is a single, unambiguous formula — not a bundle of unrelated numbers presented as one metric.
* [ ] Every supporting KPI traces to a specific persona pain point (from the Personas document) or a specific problem claim (from the Problem Statement document).
* [ ] At least 3 explicit guardrail/anti-metrics are documented with stated ceilings or exclusion rules.
* [ ] Signed off by: CEO/Founder(s) (required), Head of Product (required), Head of Data/Analytics (required once hired).
