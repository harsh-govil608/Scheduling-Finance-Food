# Document 29: Hiring Standards

## Document Name
Hiring Standards

## Purpose
Define the engineering hiring bar and interview process — what "good" looks like at each level, how interviews are structured to assess it consistently — so hiring quality doesn't degrade as the team scales from a handful of founding engineers to hundreds.

## Why It Exists
Early hires set the culture and quality bar for everyone who follows; without documented hiring standards, interview quality becomes interviewer-dependent, and the bar silently drifts (usually downward) as hiring velocity increases under growth pressure. Because this product handles financial and health data for tens of millions of users, a hiring miss isn't just a productivity cost — it's a trust and safety risk. This document exists to make the bar explicit and portable, so it survives founder involvement in every loop.

## Approximate Page Count
7-9 pages

## Sections
1. **Level Definitions & Expectations** — what's expected at each engineering level, tying directly to the Engineering Career Ladder (Phase 9 Doc 28).
2. **Interview Loop Structure** — stages (recruiter screen, technical phone screen, onsite/virtual loop panels), what each stage assesses, and how panels are composed to avoid single-interviewer bias.
3. **Values Alignment Assessment** — how the Phase 1 Guiding Principles get assessed in hiring as a first-class signal, not an afterthought, including example behavioral prompts tied to specific principles.
4. **Technical Assessment Design** — what technical interviews evaluate at each level (coding, system design, debugging), how problems are chosen to avoid leaked/memorized answers, and how AI/ML-specific and security-specific loops differ from general backend loops.
5. **Calibration Practice** — how interviewers are trained and calibrated against each other before running loops solo, including shadow/reverse-shadow requirements and periodic recalibration.
6. **Debrief & Decision Process** — how interviewer feedback is collected, the debrief meeting format, who makes the final hire/no-hire call, and how disagreement is resolved without defaulting to the most senior voice in the room.
7. **Bar-Raiser / Anti-Regression Mechanism** — a designated role or step whose job is specifically to prevent bar erosion under hiring-velocity pressure.
8. **Candidate Experience Standards** — response-time SLAs, feedback practices, and treatment standards that reflect the Guiding Principles even for candidates who aren't hired.
9. **Diversity & Sourcing Practices** — how the loop and sourcing pipeline are designed to avoid narrowing to founder networks as the company scales.

## Deliverables
- Level-by-level interview rubric mapped to the Career Ladder
- Interview loop templates per role family (backend, AI/ML, security, SRE, frontend/client)
- Calibration training program and recalibration cadence
- Debrief meeting template and decision-authority chart
- Bar-raiser program definition with named initial holders of the role

## Dependencies
Requires Engineering Career Ladder (Phase 9 Doc 28) and Guiding Principles Document (Phase 1). Coordinates with Engineering Onboarding (Phase 9 Doc 30) so the hiring loop's assessment of a candidate's gaps feeds directly into their onboarding plan.

## Teams
Engineering Leadership, VP Engineering, Head of People/HR, Recruiting, Bar-Raiser Panel, Hiring Managers

## Completion Criteria
- [ ] Interview loop piloted and calibrated across at least three interviewers before final sign-off.
- [ ] Values-alignment rubric reviewed against the Guiding Principles Document for direct traceability of each behavioral prompt.
- [ ] Bar-raiser role defined and staffed with at least one named holder per major role family.
- [ ] Candidate experience SLAs reviewed by People/HR for legal and fairness compliance.
- [ ] Signed off by: VP Engineering (required), Head of People/HR (required once hired).
