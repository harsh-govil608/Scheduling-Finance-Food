# Document 28: Engineering Career Ladder

## Document Name
Engineering Career Ladder

## Purpose
Define the leveling framework for engineers — both the individual-contributor (IC) track and the management track — including the scope, autonomy, and impact expected at each level, and the criteria used to evaluate promotion between levels. This document is the shared vocabulary the rest of Phase 9's people practices (hiring, onboarding, performance review) are written against.

## Why It Exists
Without a written ladder, "level" becomes whatever a manager privately believes it means, and two engineers doing equivalent work can land on different levels depending on who evaluates them. As the team grows from a handful of founding engineers to hundreds spread across 9+ backend services and specialized AI/ML, security, and platform functions, leveling must be consistent across teams the founders no longer directly observe. This document exists to make "what does good look like at this level" answerable the same way in every team, and to give engineers a legible path for growth that doesn't require becoming a manager to be recognized for increasing impact.

## Approximate Page Count
8-10 pages

## Sections
1. **Ladder Philosophy** — why the ladder has parallel IC and management tracks of equal standing, and how it reflects the Phase 1 Guiding Principles (e.g., valuing depth and craft, not just scope of authority).
2. **IC Track Level Definitions** — scope, technical ownership, and expected autonomy at each IC level, from new-grad/entry through distinguished/principal engineer.
3. **Management Track Level Definitions** — scope of people and org responsibility at each management level, from team lead through director-equivalent, and how it diverges from the IC track above senior levels.
4. **Track Transfer Mechanics** — how an engineer moves from IC to management or back, including the evaluation used and how prior level maps across tracks.
5. **Impact Dimensions Per Level** — the axes leveling is judged on at each level (technical scope, execution ownership, cross-team influence, mentorship) and how they're weighted differently by level.
6. **Promotion Process & Cadence** — how promotion packets are built, who reviews them, the calibration step across managers, and how often promotion cycles run.
7. **Leveling for Specialized Tracks** — how the ladder applies to AI/ML engineers, SRE/reliability engineers, and security engineers, whose impact doesn't always look like shipping product features.
8. **Compensation Banding Linkage** — how each level maps to a compensation band, without exposing the compensation-setting process itself (owned elsewhere) to keep this document leveling-focused.
9. **Leveling at Scale** — how leveling calibration is preserved as the org grows past the point where a single VP Engineering has seen every promotion case personally.

## Deliverables
- Published ladder document with explicit level definitions for both tracks, versioned and discoverable
- Impact-dimension rubric usable by managers to write promotion packets
- Promotion process workflow with named reviewers and calibration step
- Track-transfer criteria and worked example
- Mapping table from level to compensation band (referencing, not duplicating, compensation documentation)

## Dependencies
Requires Guiding Principles Document (Phase 1) for the values the ladder must reflect. Directly precedes and is required by Hiring Standards (Phase 9 Doc 29), which maps interview assessment to these level definitions. Referenced by Engineering Onboarding (Phase 9 Doc 30) for framing a new engineer's starting level, and by performance-review and compensation processes defined elsewhere in Phase 9.

## Teams
Engineering Leadership, VP Engineering, Head of People/HR, Engineering Managers, Staff/Principal Engineer Council

## Completion Criteria
- [ ] IC and management track level definitions reviewed and approved by at least three engineering managers across different teams for consistency.
- [ ] Specialized-track leveling (AI/ML, SRE, Security) validated with the respective functional leads.
- [ ] Promotion process piloted on at least one real promotion packet before publication.
- [ ] Compensation banding linkage cross-checked with People/HR compensation owner.
- [ ] Signed off by: VP Engineering (required), Head of People/HR (required once hired), CEO/Founder (required for initial ladder version).
