# Document 40: Future Expansion Strategy

## Document Name
Future Expansion Strategy

## Purpose

Describe, at the level of product vision rather than delivery planning, the directions the product could plausibly grow into — additional pillars beyond Productivity, Finance, and Health, additional interaction modalities, and additional platform surfaces. This document is a set of illustrative, non-committal directions and the test any of them must pass, not a dated roadmap and not a fundraising narrative.

## Why It Exists

A product built around "one intelligent assistant, three pillars" will inevitably surface adjacent domains users want folded in — relationships, learning, career, home — and without a written frame for evaluating those directions, teams either foreclose reasonable expansion too early (treating the three-pillar architecture as permanently fixed) or overreach into speculative surfaces that dilute the "one assistant" coherence the mission depends on. This document exists so any future conversation about "should we become a fourth-pillar / new-modality / new-platform product" starts from an agreed test and a shared list of invariants, rather than from scratch or from whatever is most fundable that quarter.

## Approximate Page Count

6-8 pages.

## Sections

1. **Expansion Philosophy** — the guiding test for whether a new domain deserves consideration as a pillar or surface at all: it must be governable by the same Remember-Predict-Suggest-Remind-Learn-Adapt-Encourage loop from the Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document, not merely "a thing users also want tracked."
2. **Candidate Future Pillars (Illustrative, Non-Committal)** — a directional, explicitly-not-decided list of domains adjacent to Productivity, Finance, and Health that share the same behavioral shape (e.g., relationships/social commitments, learning/skill development, home/household management), framed as illustrations of the test in Section 1, not as a commitment.
3. **New Modality Directions** — product-level description (not technical feasibility) of how the assistant could be experienced differently than a mobile app screen — e.g., predominantly voice-first, ambient/glanceable, wearable-driven — and what would have to remain true of the behavioral loop in each.
4. **New Platform Surface Directions** — product-surface description of where the assistant could show up beyond the primary mobile app (desktop companion, wearable, smart-home surface, browser presence), described as "what it would feel like to meet the assistant there," not as an engineering or infrastructure assessment.
5. **Household and Multi-Person Expansion** — how the single-user assumption underlying the current product could someday extend toward shared or family context, described experientially (what it would feel like for two people to share an assistant) without specifying data model or technical mechanics.
6. **Invariants — What Must Not Change** — the fixed points that must survive any expansion direction: the one-assistant feel across all surfaces, the Proactivity Ladder and "never overwhelm" ceiling from the Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document, and the anti-dark-pattern stance from the Guiding Principles Document.
7. **Signals Worth Taking Seriously** — qualitative signals (not dates or targets) that would justify seriously evaluating a given expansion direction, e.g., a sustained, unprompted user request pattern, or existing cross-pillar data repeatedly pointing at an adjacent unmet need.
8. **Explicit Non-Scope** — states plainly that this document contains no dated roadmap, no committed sequencing, no fundraising or investor narrative, and no engineering feasibility or resourcing assessment; those belong to later planning and business-model phases.

## Deliverables

* Approved Future Expansion Strategy document.
* A one-page "expansion test" checklist usable whenever a new pillar, modality, or platform idea is proposed internally.
* A living, explicitly-labeled-as-illustrative list of candidate directions, kept separate from any committed roadmap artifact.

## Dependencies

Requires the Vision & Mission Document (Phase 1) as the north star any expansion direction must still serve, the Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document (Phase 1) for the behavioral-loop test and the invariants that must survive expansion, the Guiding Principles Document (Phase 1) for the anti-dark-pattern invariant, and the Product Pillars Overview (Document 2) as the baseline pillar definition any "new pillar" candidate is compared against.

## Which Teams Use This

Product, Executive leadership, Design, Data Science/ML.

## Completion Criteria

- [ ] Expansion test in Section 1 has been applied to every candidate listed in Section 2, with a stated rationale for why each passes the test (not just an assertion that it does).
- [ ] Every invariant in Section 6 traces back to a named Phase 1 or Phase 2 document rather than being newly invented.
- [ ] Document contains zero dates, quarters, funding rounds, or committed sequencing of any kind.
- [ ] Confirmed with at least one reviewer outside Product (e.g., Design or Data Science/ML) that no listed direction reads as a de facto commitment.
- [ ] Signed off by: CEO/Founder(s) (required), Head of Product (required).
