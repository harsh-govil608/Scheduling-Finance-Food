# Document 38: Premium Experience

## Document Name
Premium Experience

## Purpose

Define the experiential difference between the free and premium tiers of the product — what the AI does more of, sooner, or more deeply for a premium user, described entirely in terms of what the user perceives and feels while using the product. This document does not define price points, packaging, billing mechanics, or conversion funnels; it defines the felt contrast between "using the assistant" and "using the fuller assistant."

## Why It Exists

The product's core promise is one intelligent assistant that remembers, predicts, suggests, reminds, learns, adapts, encourages, and never overwhelms — the moment a paywall makes the free tier feel deliberately hobbled (a nagging, half-working assistant designed to frustrate rather than help), the product violates its own Guiding Principles stance against dark patterns and starts competing with its own mission. Because Productivity, Finance, and Health are experienced as one assistant rather than three apps, a tier boundary drawn inconsistently across pillars (e.g., Finance fully gated, Health fully open) would fracture the "one assistant" feeling the Product Pillars Overview and Product Philosophy Document depend on. This document exists so every pillar team and every growth initiative implements the same free/premium contrast, anchored to the same philosophy, instead of each team drawing its own paywall line.

## Approximate Page Count

7-9 pages.

## Sections

1. **Premium Experience Philosophy** — the core stance: premium expands the depth, foresight, and autonomy of the same assistant; it never repairs a free experience that was deliberately degraded to create upgrade pressure.
2. **Free Tier Completeness Bar** — the non-negotiable floor: what a free user must be able to do, unprompted and unnagged, across all three pillars, for the free tier to count as a genuinely useful assistant rather than a demo.
3. **Proactivity Ladder Access by Tier** — how each tier maps onto the Proactivity Ladder defined in the Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document (e.g., free tier caps at suggestion-only rungs; premium unlocks higher-autonomy rungs), and why autonomy — not raw feature count — is the primary premium differentiator.
4. **Depth-of-Foresight Differences per Pillar** — what specifically feels deeper at premium in Productivity (e.g., longer prediction horizon), Finance (e.g., richer budget foresight), and Health (e.g., richer pattern insight), described as user-perceived capability, not as a feature checklist.
5. **The Upgrade Moment — Tone and Trigger Rules** — when and how the product ever raises the idea of premium: it must read as a natural extension of a moment of realized value, never as an interruption, guilt trip, or artificially manufactured scarcity; explicit contrast against dark-pattern upgrade prompts named in the Guiding Principles Document.
6. **Downgrade and Cancellation Experience** — what a user feels when they downgrade: data, history, and trust already built with the AI are preserved and never used punitively; the assistant becomes narrower, not resentful or degraded in tone.
7. **What Premium Never Changes** — invariants that hold at every tier regardless of payment: explainability of automated actions, the anti-guilt/anti-shaming stance carried from the Guiding Principles Document, and the "never overwhelm" ceiling on proactive interruptions.
8. **Cross-Pillar Consistency Check** — a method for verifying the premium boundary feels like one coherent upgrade of one assistant, not three independently gated apps bundled together.
9. **Explicit Non-Scope** — states plainly that pricing, packaging, discounting, trial mechanics, and business-model economics are owned by a later business-model phase and are not defined in this document.

## Deliverables

* Approved Premium Experience document.
* A single-page "Free Tier Completeness Bar" checklist usable by any feature team to confirm a feature does not violate the free-tier floor.
* A tier-to-Proactivity-Ladder mapping diagram, cross-referenced with the Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document.

## Dependencies

Requires the Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document (Phase 1) for the Proactivity Ladder this document maps tiers onto, and the Guiding Principles Document (Phase 1) for the anti-dark-pattern stance that bounds every upgrade moment. Requires the Product Pillars Overview (Document 2) so tier differences are checked for consistency across Productivity, Finance, and Health.

## Which Teams Use This

Product, Design, Growth, Data Science/ML, Trust & Safety, Content/Copy.

## Completion Criteria

- [ ] Free Tier Completeness Bar is defined concretely enough that a feature team can test a specific feature against it and get an unambiguous pass/fail.
- [ ] Every premium capability is mapped to a specific rung of the Proactivity Ladder from the Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document.
- [ ] Upgrade-moment rules have been checked against the Guiding Principles Document's anti-dark-pattern stance with at least two hypothetical upgrade prompts evaluated.
- [ ] Downgrade/cancellation experience explicitly states that user data and history are never stripped or degraded punitively.
- [ ] Confirmed this document contains no pricing figures, packaging tiers-as-SKUs, or business-model mechanics.
- [ ] Signed off by: Head of Product (required), Head of Growth (required), Head of Design (required).
