# Document 36: Accessibility

## Document Name
Accessibility

## Purpose

Define the product's accessibility commitments and experience requirements across visual, motor, cognitive, and hearing modalities, applied consistently across all three pillars — Productivity, Finance, and Health.

## Why It Exists

A product designed to manage a person's whole life — their schedule, their money, their health — has a heightened obligation to be usable by people across the full range of ability, since being excluded from this product means being excluded from tools that manage genuinely consequential parts of daily life. Left undefined, accessibility becomes whatever each pillar team happens to remember to add at the end of a feature, and the product's own "Never Overwhelm" and plain-language philosophy — which already overlaps heavily with cognitive accessibility — will be implemented inconsistently or not at all. This document exists to make accessibility a stated product requirement evaluated at the same stage as any other product decision, not a compliance afterthought bolted on before launch.

## Approximate Page Count

6-8 pages.

## Sections

1. **Accessibility Commitment & Standards Baseline** — the product-level accessibility standard the team commits to meeting, stated as experience goals rather than technical conformance criteria.
2. **Visual Accessibility Requirements** — experience-level requirements for contrast, text scaling, and screen-reader-usable content across all three pillars.
3. **Motor Accessibility Requirements** — requirements for tap-target sizing, gesture alternatives, and one-handed or limited-dexterity use.
4. **Cognitive Accessibility Requirements** — requirements tied directly to the proactive nature of this AI: plain language, reduced cognitive load, predictable interaction patterns, and explicit alignment with the Never Overwhelm principle.
5. **Hearing Accessibility Requirements** — requirements for captioning or visual equivalents of any audio, voice, or spoken-notification interaction.
6. **Pillar-Specific Accessibility Scenarios** — one worked scenario per pillar (Productivity, Finance, Health) showing how a user with a specific accessibility need completes a core task.
7. **Assistive Technology Compatibility Expectations** — the product-level expectation that the experience works with common assistive technologies, stated without prescribing engineering implementation.
8. **Accessibility Review Cadence** — the product process for when and how accessibility is re-evaluated as new features ship, distinct from one-time launch compliance.

## Deliverables

* Approved Accessibility document.
* An accessibility requirements checklist organized by modality (visual, motor, cognitive, hearing).
* One worked accessibility scenario per pillar.
* A recurring accessibility review process description for product and design use.

## Dependencies

Requires the Guiding Principles Document (Phase 1) for the product's baseline ethical commitments; requires the User Personas Document (Phase 1) for the range of user needs represented; requires the Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document (Phase 1) for the Never Overwhelm principle underlying the cognitive accessibility requirements.

## Which Teams Use This

Product, Design, Content/Copy, Engineering (as downstream consumers), Trust & Safety, Legal/Compliance (as downstream consumers).

## Completion Criteria

- [ ] Every modality (visual, motor, cognitive, hearing) has at least one concrete, testable requirement.
- [ ] Each of the three pillars has a validated worked accessibility scenario.
- [ ] Cognitive accessibility requirements have been cross-checked against the Never Overwhelm principle for consistency.
- [ ] The accessibility review cadence has been agreed upon by Product and Design as a recurring process, not a one-time gate.
- [ ] Signed off by: Head of Product (required), Head of Design (required).
