# Phase 2 — Product Definition Requirements

Per `phase2.md`, this is the **Requirements Specification for Phase 2**, not the Product Definition itself — exactly as `requirements.md` was the spec for Phase 1. It defines every document needed to completely answer "what is the product" before any engineering architecture, UI implementation, AI implementation, or coding begins.

Phase 1 (Company Foundation — Vision & Mission, Product Philosophy, Problem Statement, Market Definition, User Personas, Success Metrics, Guiding Principles) defined **why** the company exists. Phase 2 defines **what** the product is.

## 4. Estimated Total Page Count

## 5. Critical Path

Documents that must exist before others can be meaningfully written (blocking dependencies, not just topical relevance):

1. **01 Product Architecture Overview** — nothing else in Phase 2 can be positioned without the map-of-maps existing first.
2. **02 Product Pillars Overview** → **03 Information Architecture** — the entity model every pillar document (16–26) and every interaction document (27–30) references.
3. **08 Automation Philosophy** — the product-level translation of the Phase 1 Proactivity Ladder that Notification System (14), User Control Model (34), Premium Experience (38), and Help & Support (41) all depend on directly.
4. **14 Notification System** — the single biggest bottleneck in the graph: every pillar document and every interaction-modality document eventually needs to arbitrate attention through this document's rules.
5. **19 Habit Tracking Experience** — must exist before 39 Gamification Philosophy can resolve the streak-shaming tension it names.
6. **26 Cross-Pillar Coordination Experience** — the proof-of-concept document for "one assistant, not three apps"; depends on all eleven Group 3 documents and is itself a dependency for parts of Group 4 and Group 6.

Everything else can be parallelized across teams once these six are stable.

## 6. Review of Completeness

Cross-checked against every item in `phase2.md`'s "Required Coverage" list (33 items): all 33 are covered by at least one document above. The following were added beyond the explicit list because they make the Product Definition substantially stronger and their absence would be a real gap in a coverage-complete set: Product Architecture Overview, Product Pillars Overview, Information Architecture, Navigation Philosophy, User Journey Map, Cross-Pillar Coordination Experience, Help & Support Experience, Localization & Regional Adaptation Experience.

## Principal Product Architect Self-Review

**Coverage assessment: ~99% complete for a single-user, mobile-first initial product scope.**

What remains deliberately unaddressed, and why:

* **Multi-user / shared or family experiences** (shared household budgets, shared task lists between partners) — not covered. This is a genuine gap *if* multi-user is in scope for initial launch, but nothing in Phase 1's Vision & Mission, Personas, or Market Definition commits to multi-user as a launch requirement — the product as defined is a single-user personal assistant. **Recommendation:** if multi-user is intended pre-launch (not just as a Future Expansion candidate), add a dedicated "Shared & Multi-User Experience" document before Phase 2 is marked final; otherwise this is correctly deferred to Document 40 (Future Expansion Strategy).
* **Legal/policy notice presentation** (how ToS, privacy policy, and required disclosures are surfaced in-product) — touched only tangentially by Permissions & Consent UX (32). This is intentionally left thin here because the substantive content of those policies belongs to the Phase 6 Trust, Safety, Privacy & Compliance phase; Phase 2 should only define *where and how* they're surfaced, which 32 already does at the boundary. Acceptable as-is.
* **Deep wearable-specific interaction patterns** (beyond generic continuity) — covered at the "what must feel continuous" level in Cross-Device Experience (29), not at the depth of a dedicated wearable UX document. Acceptable for this stage; worth revisiting once a specific wearable platform is prioritized.
* **Notification System (14) and Automation Philosophy (08) are the two highest-leverage documents in the entire set** — any drift in quality there propagates into roughly 30 downstream documents. These two should receive disproportionate review rigor (multiple reviewers, adversarial read) relative to their page count.

No other item from `phase2.md`'s Required Coverage list, and no additional product-domain decision the reviewer could identify, remains unspecified. Phase 2's document architecture is ready to move to drafting.
