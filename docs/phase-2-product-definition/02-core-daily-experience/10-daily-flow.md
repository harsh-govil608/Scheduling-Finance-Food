# Document 10: Daily Flow

## Document Name
Daily Flow

## Purpose

Define the recurring rhythm of an average day of use — how morning, midday, evening, and night connect into one continuous experience — and set the product-level rules for when the AI proactively surfaces something versus deliberately stays silent, across Productivity, Finance, and Health. This document is the connective tissue between the single Morning Experience and Night Summary Experience documents and the moment-to-moment behavior in between.

## Why It Exists

Without a shared daily rhythm, each pillar will design its own daily loop in isolation — a Health team building a midday nudge, a Finance team building a midday spend alert, a Productivity team building a midday priority check — all firing independently and producing a day that feels like three separate apps competing for the same ten seconds of attention. This document exists so the daily cadence is designed once, as a single day shared by all three pillars, directly operationalizing the "one assistant, not three apps" and "never overwhelm" commitments from the Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document.

## Approximate Page Count

7-9 pages.

## Sections

1. **The Daily Arc Structure** — the segmentation of a day (morning, midday, evening, night) and what categories of behavior belong to each segment.
2. **Silence as a Designed State** — the rules for when the AI deliberately produces no output at all, and why silence must be treated as an intentional, specified state rather than an absence of design.
3. **Pillar Rotation & Attention Budget** — the daily-level philosophy for how Finance, Health, and Productivity share a single attention budget across a day rather than each pillar independently competing for it; detailed arbitration mechanics are owned by the Notification System document.
4. **Micro-Moments vs. Anchor Moments** — the distinction between small ambient touches (e.g., a water reminder) and the day's fixed anchor moments (the morning briefing, the night summary), and the rule that anchors must never be crowded out by micro-moments.
5. **Context Shifts Within a Day** — the behavioral expectations for how the AI adjusts as the user's context changes (commute, work hours, meals, workouts); the underlying detection mechanics belong to the Context Engine — Product Perspective document.
6. **Weekday vs. Weekend Rhythm** — how the daily flow rules differ for structured weekdays versus unstructured weekends or off-days.
7. **Handling an Uneventful Day** — the specified behavior when no pillar has anything urgent to surface, so the AI does not manufacture false urgency to justify its presence.
8. **Handling an Overloaded Day** — the specified behavior when multiple pillars simultaneously need attention on the same day, deferring to the Notification System document for the detailed arbitration table.
9. **Daily Flow Diagram Requirements** — the specification for a single "day-in-the-life" diagram spanning all three pillars, to be produced as a companion artifact.

## Deliverables

* Approved Daily Flow document.
* A single "day-in-the-life" diagram spanning morning through night, annotated across all three pillars.
* A table distinguishing anchor moments from micro-moments, usable as a reference by every pillar feature team.

## Dependencies

Requires the **Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document** (the Behavioral Loop and Proactivity Ladder govern what may happen and when), the **Context Engine — Product Perspective** document (context-shift detection), the **Automation Philosophy** document (how much of the day's rhythm is automated versus user-initiated), and the **User Journey Map** (Document 9, this day sits inside that longer arc).

## Which Teams Use This

Product, Design, Data Science/ML, Engineering.

## Completion Criteria

- [ ] Every segment of the daily arc (morning, midday, evening, night) has explicit rules for both what may be surfaced and when silence is required.
- [ ] The pillar rotation / attention-budget philosophy has been validated against at least one uneventful-day and one overloaded-day scenario.
- [ ] Weekday and weekend rhythm differences are each documented with a concrete example.
- [ ] The day-in-the-life diagram has been reviewed against the Morning Experience and Night Summary Experience documents for consistency at the handoff points.
- [ ] Signed off by: Head of Product (required), Head of Design (required).
