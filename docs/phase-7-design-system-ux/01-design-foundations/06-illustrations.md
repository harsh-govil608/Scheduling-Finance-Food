# Document 06: Illustrations

## Document Name
Illustrations

## Purpose
Define the platform's illustration system — the style, tone, and usage rules for illustrations used in empty states, onboarding, and moments of encouragement — so illustration consistently reinforces a calm, supportive emotional register rather than being treated as decoration bolted on late in a feature's design.

## Why It Exists
Illustrations appear at some of the most emotionally sensitive moments in the product: a first-run onboarding screen, an empty task list, a "no data yet" state on a health or finance dashboard, or a congratulatory moment after a goal is met. These are exactly the moments where the Phase 1 "encourage, never overwhelm" philosophy is most visible and most easily undermined by an illustration style that is too clinical, too juvenile, or inconsistent with the rest of the visual language. Without a defined illustration system, individual designers or outsourced illustrators will default to generic stock-style art that feels disconnected from the product's calm, trustworthy identity, weakening the emotional payoff of encouragement moments and making empty states feel like errors rather than natural pauses.

## Approximate Page Count
6-8 pages

## Sections
1. **Illustration Style & Selection Criteria** — the visual criteria (line quality, color usage, level of abstraction, warmth) an illustration style must satisfy, not a final commissioned style.
2. **Relationship to Encourage, Never Overwhelm** — how illustration tone specifically operationalizes the Phase 1 philosophy at emotionally sensitive moments.
3. **Empty State Illustrations** — usage rules for illustrations that appear when a user has no data yet, ensuring the state reads as a calm invitation rather than an error.
4. **Onboarding Illustrations** — style and sequencing rules for illustrations used to welcome and orient new users across the three pillars.
5. **Encouragement & Milestone Moments** — rules for celebratory or supportive illustrations tied to goals met, streaks, or recovery from a setback, calibrated to avoid feeling patronizing or excessive.
6. **Per-Pillar Illustration Variation** — how illustration subject matter can vary across Productivity, Finance, and Health while remaining stylistically unified with the rest of the design language.
7. **Color & Motion Integration** — how illustrations draw from the Color System and where limited motion (e.g., a subtle entrance animation) is appropriate.
8. **Accessibility Considerations** — requirements for illustrations to never carry meaning alone, appropriate alt-text/description guidance, and consideration of reduced-motion settings.
9. **Illustration Library Governance** — process for commissioning, reviewing, and adding new illustrations so style consistency holds as the library grows.
10. **Usage Examples & Anti-Patterns** — annotated examples of appropriate illustration use versus overuse or emotionally mismatched use.

## Deliverables
- Illustration style selection criteria (not a final commissioned art style).
- Usage rules for empty states, onboarding, and encouragement/milestone moments.
- Per-pillar variation guidance within one unified style.
- Accessibility guidance covering alt-text and reduced-motion behavior.
- Illustration library governance/commissioning process.

## Dependencies
