# Document 01: Design Principles

## Document Name
Design Principles

## Purpose
Define the small set of visual and interaction design principles that every screen, component, and micro-interaction in the product must be judged against. These principles translate the company's behavioral philosophy ("encouraging not guilting, calm not overwhelming") into concrete, checkable rules that designers and engineers apply at the pixel and interaction level.

## Why It Exists
Phase 1's Guiding Principles govern the company as a whole, and Phase 2's Product Philosophy Document defines the tone the assistant should strike in its behavior and language. Neither tells a designer what to do when a screen has too many elements competing for attention, whether an animation should ease in over 200ms or 400ms, or how to signal an overdue bill without producing shame. Without a dedicated, visually-oriented principle set, individual designers and the three pillar teams (Productivity, Finance, Health) will each interpret "calm" and "encouraging" differently, producing visually inconsistent screens that undermine the "one assistant" mission and reintroduce the anxiety-inducing patterns the product exists to remove. This document exists to give every design decision — including ones made quickly, under deadline pressure, or by a new hire who has not internalized the company culture — a fast, objective test to check against.

## Approximate Page Count
8-10 pages

## Sections
1. **Relationship to Company & Product Philosophy** — how these visual/interaction principles operationalize Phase 1's Guiding Principles and Phase 2's Product Philosophy Document without duplicating them.
2. **Calm by Default** — rules for visual density, motion restraint, and notification volume that keep the interface from ever feeling like a dashboard of alarms.
3. **Clarity Over Cleverness** — preference for legible, predictable UI patterns over novel interactions; criteria for when novelty is and is not earned.
4. **One-Glance Comprehension** — requirements for a user to understand their most important status (financial, health, task) within a few seconds of opening the app, without deciphering.
5. **Encourage, Never Guilt** — visual and copy-adjacent rules for how warnings, overdue items, and missed goals are presented so they motivate rather than shame.
6. **Proactive, Not Intrusive** — principles governing how the assistant surfaces unprompted suggestions visually (placement, weight, dismissibility) so proactivity reads as helpful rather than nagging.
7. **One Assistant, Three Pillars** — the visual cohesion rule ensuring Productivity, Finance, and Health never look or feel like three different products.
8. **Accessibility as Default, Not Add-On** — principle that accessible design is a first-class constraint on every decision, not a pass applied afterward.
9. **Trust Through Consistency** — principle that predictable, repeated visual patterns build user trust, especially for sensitive financial and health data.
10. **Principle Conflict Resolution** — guidance for what to do when two principles pull in different directions (e.g., clarity vs. calm) and who arbitrates.

## Deliverables
- A ranked or grouped list of 6-10 named design principles, each with a one-line definition, a rationale, and a "do / don't" visual or interaction example.
- A quick-reference checklist format usable in design reviews.
- Documented relationship/non-overlap statement versus Phase 1 Guiding Principles and Phase 2 Product Philosophy Document.
- Escalation/arbitration process for principle conflicts.

## Dependencies
Requires Phase 1 Guiding Principles and Phase 2 Doc 02 Product Philosophy Document as the source of the tone these principles express visually; informs and is a prerequisite for all other Phase 7 documents, beginning with Design Language (Phase 7).

## Teams
Design, Product, Brand, Engineering

## Completion Criteria
- [ ] Every principle has a testable do/don't example, not just an abstract statement.
- [ ] Document explicitly reconciled against Phase 1 Guiding Principles and Phase 2 Product Philosophy Document with no unexplained contradictions.
- [ ] Principle conflict-resolution process reviewed by Product.
- [ ] Signed off by: Head of Design (required), VP Product (required), Brand Lead (consulted).
