# Document 18: Wearable Partnerships

## Document Name
Wearable Partnerships

## Purpose
Define the business strategy for wearable-platform partnerships — which manufacturer APIs and OS-level health aggregators to pursue as formal partners versus integrate against as open public APIs, what commercial and certification terms to seek, and how these relationships extend the Health pillar's passive-signal capture beyond a bare technical connection. This document applies the Partnerships (General Strategy, Doc 16) framework to a category defined not by a single dominant vendor but by a fragmented, multi-platform ecosystem, where the central business question is prioritization and sequencing rather than negotiating leverage over one counterparty.

## Why It Exists
Phase 4's Wearables document (Doc 49) defines the technical normalization architecture needed to ingest sleep, workout, and vitals data across many different manufacturer and OS-level platforms, and Phase 7's Wearables document (Doc 24) defines how the assistant renders information back on-wrist — but neither document decides which wearable platforms are worth the business investment of a formal partner or developer-program relationship, in what order, or under what terms. Unlike banking, no single wearable partner is required for the Health pillar to function, which creates a different failure mode: without a deliberate sequencing strategy, engineering effort gets spread thin chasing every platform at once, or the company under-invests in the platform its actual user base owns. This document exists to turn "which wearables do we support" from an engineering-capacity question into a business-prioritized roadmap, and to capture the certification, co-marketing, and data-sharing terms available when a relationship moves from a bare public API integration to a formal partnership.

## Approximate Page Count
6-8 pages

## Sections
1. **Partner Selection Criteria** — what makes a wearable platform worth pursuing as a formal partner rather than a bare public-API integration: installed base among the target user personas (Phase 1, Doc 05), API stability and support quality, and data-sharing terms compatible with the Phase 1 anti-data-selling principle.
2. **Commercial Deal Structure** — the deal archetypes typical of this category (free reciprocal developer-program membership, co-marketing exchange, certification/"Works With" licensing fees) as distinct from the revenue-share or flat-fee models more common in Banking Partnerships (Doc 17), and why wearable platforms rarely support a paid data-access model.
3. **Certification & Developer Program Participation** — the business case for pursuing official partner or developer-program certification (badging, priority API access, early access to platform changes) versus remaining an unofficial integrator, evaluated per platform.
4. **Multi-Platform Coverage & Sequencing Strategy** — the prioritization logic for which wearable ecosystems receive formal partnership investment first, given constrained business-development and engineering capacity and a fragmented market with no single dominant player.
5. **Partnership Dependency & Fragmentation Risk** — the business risk profile specific to this category: no single point of failure the way a sole banking partner would be, but a compounding integration-maintenance burden as the number of formally supported platforms grows, and the criteria for deciding when to sunset support for a low-adoption platform.
6. **Data-Sharing Terms & Anti-Data-Selling Compliance** — the specific contractual and platform-policy considerations for health and biometric data, which typically carry stricter platform-level data-use restrictions than general integration data, reconciled against the Phase 1 anti-data-selling principle.
7. **Co-Marketing & Distribution Opportunities** — how a formal wearable partnership can unlock app-store or health-platform-marketplace featuring, joint launch marketing, or bundled promotion, and how this feeds Growth (Phase 8) acquisition channels.
8. **Partner Relationship Management & API Change Escalation** — the business-level relationship needed to get advance notice of breaking API changes or deprecations from a wearable platform, and the escalation path when a platform partner degrades support without notice.
9. **Emerging Adjacent Partnership Categories** — how the wearable data foundation could open future partnership categories building on it (corporate wellness platforms, health insurers offering premium discounts for verified activity), flagged directionally without committing to pursue them in the current phase.

## Deliverables
- Partner shortlist with evaluation scorecards against the selection criteria, prioritized by installed base among target personas.
- Commercial deal structure guidance per platform category (manufacturer API vs. OS-level health aggregator).
- Certification/developer-program roadmap with a go/no-go recommendation per platform.
- Multi-platform coverage and sequencing plan aligned to engineering capacity from Phase 4 Doc 49.
- Data-sharing compliance checklist specific to health/biometric data terms.
- Co-marketing opportunity brief for Growth (Phase 8).
- Partner relationship management plan with an API-change escalation path per formally partnered platform.
