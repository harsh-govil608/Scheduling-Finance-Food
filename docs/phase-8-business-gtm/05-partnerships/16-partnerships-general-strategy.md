# Document 16: Partnerships (General Strategy)

## Document Name
Partnerships (General Strategy)

## Purpose
Define the umbrella framework the company uses to evaluate, structure, and govern every business partnership underneath a technical integration or distribution relationship — a single partner evaluation rubric, commercial deal-structure playbook, and governance model applied consistently across categories such as banking, wearables, calendar providers, and health platforms. This document is the strategic parent from which the category-specific partnership documents (Banking Partnerships, Doc 17; Wearable Partnerships, Doc 18; and future categories) are derived, so each category is negotiated against a shared standard rather than reinvented deal-by-deal.

## Why It Exists
Phase 4 committed the product to depending on numerous external technical integrations (Docs 42-49) and Phase 6 Doc 30 defined how those vendors are assessed for security and privacy risk, but neither document defines the business side of the relationship — how a partner is selected among competing options, what commercial terms the company should push for, who has authority to sign, and how partnership performance is tracked after launch. Without a shared framework, each category team negotiates partnerships independently: one team might accept an exclusivity clause that quietly locks out a better future partner, another might agree to data-sharing terms that violate the Phase 1 anti-data-selling guiding principle, and a third might have no plan for what happens if the partner exits. This document exists to give every partnership category — present and future — a single, reusable evaluation and negotiation standard, so the specific playbooks in Doc 17 and Doc 18 are specializations of one strategy rather than unrelated one-off deals.

## Approximate Page Count
8-10 pages

## Sections
1. **Partnership Category Taxonomy** — the classes of partnership the company enters into (data/integration partners such as banking and wearables; distribution partners such as app stores, device OEMs, and telecom or employer bundles; ecosystem/co-marketing partners), and why each class is governed by this same umbrella framework.
2. **Universal Partner Evaluation Framework** — the scoring rubric applied to any prospective partner regardless of category: strategic fit, technical feasibility, commercial terms, data-sharing compatibility with the Phase 1 anti-data-selling principle, and reputational risk.
3. **Commercial Deal Structure Playbook** — the general reasoning for choosing among deal archetypes (revenue share, flat fee, free reciprocal integration, co-marketing exchange) and the conditions under which each archetype is appropriate, independent of category.
4. **Build vs. Partner vs. Buy Decision Framework** — the criteria used to decide whether a capability should be delivered through a formal partnership, built entirely in-house, or acquired, so "partner" is a deliberate choice rather than a default.
5. **Partnership Governance & Negotiation Authority** — who owns a partner relationship end-to-end (Business Development), the approval workflow for entering a partnership, and the tiered signing-authority limits (e.g., which deals a BD lead can close versus which require CEO/Executive sign-off).
6. **Partnership Risk Tiering (Business Layer)** — the business-level risk lens applied on top of Phase 6 Doc 30's security/vendor risk tiering: commercial dependency concentration, exclusivity lock-in, and single-partner-of-record risk per category.
7. **Partnership Lifecycle Management** — the stages every partnership moves through (sourcing, evaluation, negotiation, onboarding, ongoing relationship management, renewal or renegotiation, termination) and the standard cadence and owner for each stage.
8. **Exclusivity & Competitive Dynamics** — the reasoning framework for when an exclusive partnership is worth the strategic trade-off versus keeping a category multi-partner, and how exclusivity decisions are reconciled against the Phase 8 competitive-positioning strategy.
9. **Category Specialization Model** — how this umbrella framework is intended to specialize into category-specific documents (Banking Partnerships, Doc 17; Wearable Partnerships, Doc 18), and the standard section structure future category playbooks (e.g., calendar providers, health platforms) should follow to stay consistent with this framework.
10. **Partnership Success Metrics** — how partnership performance is measured post-launch (user activation lift attributable to the partner, retention impact, integration cost versus value delivered, partner relationship health), and the review cadence for deciding whether to expand, hold, or exit a partnership.

## Deliverables
- Universal partner evaluation rubric usable by any category team without modification.
- Commercial deal structure playbook with archetype selection guidance.
- Build vs. partner vs. buy decision framework with worked decision tree.
- Partnership governance model, including a RACI for sourcing, negotiation, signing authority, and ongoing management.
- Business-layer partnership risk tiering matrix, cross-referenced to Phase 6 Doc 30's vendor risk tiers.
- Partnership lifecycle process document with stage owners and standard cadences.
- Partnership success metrics framework and review-cadence template.

## Dependencies
Requires Business Model (Phase 8, Doc 01), Guiding Principles (Phase 1, Doc 07), Third-Party Risk Management (Phase 6, Doc 30), and awareness of the full Phase 4 integrations set (Docs 42-49) as the technical surface partnerships sit underneath. Feeds directly into Banking Partnerships (Doc 17) and Wearable Partnerships (Doc 18), and is intended to be reused by future category-specific partnership documents (e.g., calendar providers, health platforms) not yet written.
