# Document 02: Pricing Strategy

## Document Name
Pricing Strategy

## Purpose
Define the methodology and reasoning framework the company will use to arrive at prices — not the final price points themselves. This document specifies how value-based, cost-plus, and competitor-based pricing logics apply (or don't) to an AI Life Operating System, and establishes the decision framework that Subscription Plans (Doc 03) will later apply to produce actual tiers.

## Why It Exists
Pricing an AI product that spans three life domains is unusually hard: there is no direct competitor selling the same bundle, the marginal cost structure is unfamiliar (inference cost per user varies with usage in a way traditional SaaS pricing doesn't), and the value delivered is diffuse (time, money, and health outcomes rather than a single measurable unit). Without an explicit pricing methodology, the company risks anchoring on arbitrary numbers, underpricing a high-value product, or copying point-solution competitors whose economics don't transfer. This document exists so every future pricing decision — new tiers, regional pricing, promotions — traces back to a defensible, reusable framework rather than being re-litigated from scratch each time.

## Approximate Page Count
7-9 pages

## Sections
1. **Pricing Philosophy** — the company's stance on how price should relate to value delivered, stated as a principle (e.g., "price to reflect value captured across all three pillars, not cost to serve one feature").
2. **Value-Based Pricing Framework** — how to estimate willingness-to-pay from the value creation thesis (Doc 01): time saved, money optimized, health outcomes improved, and how these are translated into a defensible value metric.
3. **Cost-Plus Considerations** — the cost structure that must be respected as a floor (AI inference cost, infrastructure, support), and how it constrains pricing without becoming the primary driver.
4. **Competitor-Based Anchoring** — how adjacent categories (productivity SaaS, personal finance apps, health/fitness subscriptions, premium AI assistants) inform price perception and anchoring, and the limits of this comparison given the bundled, cross-pillar nature of the product.
5. **Pricing Model Options Analysis** — a structured comparison of candidate pricing models (flat subscription, usage-based, tiered-feature, hybrid/freemium) against the product's cost and value profile, with a recommended direction and rationale.
6. **Willingness-to-Pay Research Plan** — the methodology for validating pricing hypotheses (surveys, van Westendorp-style price sensitivity testing, concierge/pilot pricing, cohort experiments) before committing to final numbers.
7. **Geographic & Segment Pricing Considerations** — the framework for reasoning about price variation by region/purchasing power and by user segment (e.g., students vs. professionals vs. families), without prescribing final figures.
8. **Price Change & Grandfathering Policy Framework** — the principles governing how prices may change over time and how existing subscribers are treated, designed to protect trust established in Phase 6.
9. **Discounting & Promotion Guardrails** — the boundaries within which discounts, trials, and promotions may operate so they don't drift into the manipulative dark-pattern territory ruled out in Phase 1.
10. **Pricing Governance** — who owns pricing decisions, how often pricing is revisited, and what data triggers a pricing review.

## Deliverables
- A documented pricing philosophy statement.
- A comparative analysis of pricing model options with a recommended model and rationale.
- A willingness-to-pay research plan ready for execution (not yet executed results).
- A price-change and grandfathering policy framework.
- Discounting/promotion guardrails reviewed against anti-dark-pattern principles.
