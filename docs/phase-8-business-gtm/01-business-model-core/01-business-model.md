# Document 01: Business Model

## Document Name
Business Model

## Purpose
Define the fundamental value-exchange model of the company — how the product creates value for users across the Productivity, Finance, and Health pillars, and how the company captures a portion of that value as revenue, consistent with the Phase 1 anti-goal ruling out data monetization. This document is the strategic root from which pricing, subscription structure, premium gating, and monetization strategy are all derived.

## Why It Exists
"How does this become a billion-dollar company" is the single most common investor question and the most common founder blind spot when a product's mission (proactively manage a user's life, reduce manual effort) doesn't obviously map to a revenue mechanism. Without this document, monetization gets bolted on ad hoc after the product already exists, usually badly — misaligned with the trust-first design of Phase 6 and the premium experience defined in Phase 2/3/7. This document forces the business model to be designed with the same rigor as the product itself, and to be reconciled explicitly against the company's stated anti-goals before a single price is set.

## Approximate Page Count
9-11 pages

## Sections
1. **Value Creation Thesis** — precisely what value the AI creates for the user (time reclaimed, money saved/optimized, health outcomes improved, stress reduced) across each of the three pillars, and why that value is durable rather than novelty-driven.
2. **Value Capture Mechanism** — subscription vs. transactional vs. hybrid vs. affiliate/marketplace, and why, given the anti-data-monetization constraint established in Phase 1's Guiding Principles.
3. **Business Model Canvas** — the full canvas (customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure) at a strategic level.
4. **Single-Assistant Bundling Logic** — the business rationale for selling Productivity, Finance, and Health as one unified assistant rather than three separate products, and how bundling affects willingness-to-pay and retention.
5. **Revenue Stream Taxonomy** — the categories of revenue the company will pursue (e.g., recurring subscription, one-time/transactional, B2B2C partnerships) ranked by strategic priority, explicitly excluding any data-sale or data-brokerage stream.
6. **Unit Economics Framework** — the structure (not final figures) for reasoning about customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, payback period, and contribution margin, and which levers in the model most affect each.
7. **Network Effects & Moat Logic** — how the business model itself (not just the product) compounds advantage over time — data-driven personalization moat, cross-pillar stickiness, switching costs — described in a way that stays consistent with the privacy commitments of Phase 6.
8. **Competitive Business-Model Positioning** — how this model differs structurally from point-solution competitors (a budgeting app, a to-do app, a health tracker) who each rely on narrower or more ad-dependent models.
9. **Ethical Guardrails on Monetization** — the explicit reconciliation of the business model against the Phase 1 anti-goals (no selling user data, no manipulative dark patterns), stated as binding constraints on every downstream Phase 8 document.
10. **Long-Term Business Model Evolution** — how the model is expected to mature as the company scales (e.g., from single-consumer subscription toward household plans, enterprise/B2B2C, or platform/API revenue), sequenced at a directional level.

## Deliverables
- A completed Business Model Canvas (visual + narrative).
- A written value creation and value capture thesis, reviewed against Phase 1 Guiding Principles.
- A revenue stream taxonomy with strategic prioritization and explicit exclusions.
- A unit economics framework document (structure and formulas, not final numbers).
- A moat/network-effects narrative suitable for investor-facing use.
