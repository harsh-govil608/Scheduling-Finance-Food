# Document 26: Brand & Positioning Strategy

## Document Name
Brand & Positioning Strategy

## Purpose
Define the company's brand identity and market positioning — name, verbal brand identity, and the positioning statement used consistently across marketing, PR, sales, and fundraising materials. This document specifies how the company presents itself to the outside world, distinct from Phase 7's product-visual Design Language (in-app look and feel) and Phase 1's internally-oriented Vision & Mission Document (why the company exists, for the team).

## Why It Exists
Vision & Mission (Phase 1) is written to align the founding team and early employees; it is deliberately internal, aspirational, and not written to be a punchy, repeatable external positioning statement. Design Language (Phase 7, Doc 02) governs the product's visual system — color, type, iconography — but explicitly excludes verbal brand voice for marketing and any decision about logo, name, or externally-facing personality. Without a dedicated brand document, external communication either awkwardly reuses internal mission language verbatim, or different teams (marketing, sales, PR, fundraising) independently invent inconsistent descriptions of what the company is and why it matters, fragmenting the brand at the exact moment — go-to-market — when consistency compounds trust. This document exists so every external-facing sentence about the company traces back to one deliberately authored, competitively tested source of truth.

## Approximate Page Count
6-8 pages

## Sections
1. **Positioning Statement** — the external-facing "for X, who need Y, this product is Z, unlike W" statement derived from, but distinct in register from, the internal mission; written to be quoted directly in decks, press, and landing pages.
2. **Brand Personality & Verbal Identity** — the tone, vocabulary, and voice principles for marketing copy, website content, ads, and PR — explicitly distinct from the Phase 1 in-product AI tone-of-voice principles, which govern what the assistant says to a user inside the app, not what the company says about itself.
3. **Category Narrative** — how the brand tells the "new category" story (AI Life Operating System, not a to-do app or budgeting app) externally, building on but rephrasing the Vision & Mission Document's Category Claim for press, analysts, and prospective users encountering the product for the first time.
4. **Naming & Nomenclature** — the rules governing the company name, product name, and feature/pillar naming conventions used in external communication, including guardrails against inconsistent or marketing-team-invented names for internal concepts already named in Phase 1-7 documents.
5. **Competitive Positioning Map** — where the brand sits relative to adjacent categories (productivity apps, budgeting apps, health trackers, generic AI assistants) on the dimensions that matter to the target user, and the specific language used to differentiate without disparaging.
6. **Messaging Pillars & Proof Points** — the 3-5 core claims the brand is allowed to make repeatedly (e.g., "proactive, not another app you have to check"), each backed by a concrete, defensible proof point drawn from the product's actual capabilities rather than aspirational language.
7. **Logo, Wordmark & Brand Mark Usage** — the rules for how the company's externally-facing marks are used across marketing surfaces, co-marketing/partner materials, and press, cross-referenced to but not duplicating the Phase 7 Design Language visual system used inside the product.
8. **Channel Adaptation Guidelines** — how the core positioning and voice flex (or must not flex) across channels — website, paid ads, app store listing, social, investor materials, press releases — so the brand is recognizable everywhere without being rigidly identical.
9. **Brand Guardrails & Anti-Positioning** — explicit statements of what the brand must never claim or imply (e.g., no overstated AI capability claims, no financial or medical advice framing), reconciled against the Phase 1 anti-goals and the Phase 6 trust commitments.
10. **Brand Governance & Evolution** — who owns brand decisions, how the positioning is revisited as the product and market evolve, and the review cadence and approval chain for any change to the core positioning statement.

## Deliverables
- A finalized, quotable positioning statement and one-paragraph company description.
- A verbal brand identity guide (tone, vocabulary, do/don't language examples) for marketing use.
- A competitive positioning map with supporting rationale.
- A messaging pillars document with proof points mapped to actual product capabilities.
- Brand usage guardrails reconciled against Phase 1 anti-goals and Phase 6 trust commitments.

## Dependencies
Requires Vision & Mission Document (Phase 1), Market Definition Document (Phase 1), Guiding Principles Document (Phase 1), User Personas Document (Phase 1), Design Language (Phase 7, Doc 02). Coordinates with Business Model (Phase 8, Doc 01) and informs all downstream Phase 8 marketing, growth, and partnership documents.

## Teams
Marketing, Brand, Executive, Product, Design, Legal/Communications

## Completion Criteria
- [ ] Positioning statement tested against at least 2 competitor positioning statements for differentiation.
- [ ] Verbal brand identity guide reviewed for consistency against the Phase 1 in-product AI tone-of-voice principles, confirming the two are intentionally distinct rather than accidentally conflicting.
- [ ] Every messaging pillar backed by a verifiable proof point, with no aspirational or unverifiable claims approved.
- [ ] Brand guardrails cross-checked against Phase 1 anti-goals and Phase 6 privacy/trust commitments with no open objections from Legal.
- [ ] Signed off by: CEO (required), Head of Marketing (required once hired), Head of Design (required).
