# Document 19: GTM Strategy

## Document Name
GTM Strategy

## Purpose
Define the go-to-market strategy for initial launch — target segment sequencing, channel strategy, launch positioning, and phased rollout structure — synthesizing the Market Definition Document's geographic and competitive prioritization with the User Personas Document's primary persona into an executable launch plan. This document does not restate market sizing or persona research; it converts those findings into a decision about who gets targeted first, through which channels, with what message, and in what order.

## Why It Exists
A well-defined product and a well-defined market don't automatically produce a successful launch — without a deliberate GTM strategy, limited launch budget and attention get spread thin across every plausible segment and channel simultaneously instead of concentrated where the Phase 1 research says it will actually work. This risk is sharper for this product than for a single-category app: because the "AI Life Operating System" spans Productivity, Finance, and Health, there is a constant temptation to launch a message broad enough to appeal to everyone, which historically produces a message that resonates with no one. This document exists to force a narrow, defensible launch bet — one primary persona, one or two channels, one sharp positioning statement — and to make that bet visible and reviewable before spend begins, rather than discovered after a diffuse launch underperforms.

## Approximate Page Count
8-10 pages

## Sections
1. **Launch Segment Sequencing** — which persona/segment from the Phase 1 User Personas Document is targeted first, why it is sequenced ahead of the secondary personas, and what signal would indicate a second segment should be opened up.
2. **Channel Strategy** — which acquisition channels (paid, organic/content, partnerships, referral) are prioritized for launch and why, evaluated against the CAC framework (Phase 8, Doc 07) rather than chosen by generic channel popularity.
3. **Launch Positioning & Messaging** — the specific message used at launch, derived from but sharper than the Vision & Mission Document and Product (Behavioral) Philosophy Document, expressed as the single sentence a first-time visitor must understand in under five seconds.
4. **Pricing & Packaging at Launch** — which subscription tier(s) from the Pricing Strategy (Phase 8, Doc 02) are exposed at launch, whether an introductory offer is used, and the rationale tied to the launch segment's willingness-to-pay signal from Phase 1.
5. **Pre-Launch Readiness Gates** — the non-negotiable bars (trust/safety readiness per Phase 6, minimum product completeness per Phase 3, minimum reliability per Phase 4) that must be met before a launch date is set, preventing GTM timelines from overriding product/trust readiness.
6. **Rollout Waves** — the phased structure from private beta through limited public launch to general availability, including the criteria for graduating between waves.
7. **Partnership & Channel Enablement** — how launch-relevant partnerships (e.g., UPI/bank/SMS-gateway relationships implied by the Market Definition Document's geographic prioritization) are activated in support of the launch, distinct from the longer-term partnership strategy.
8. **Competitive Response Plan** — anticipated reactions from named competitors (per the Market Definition Document's Competitive Landscape) and the prepared response if a competitor moves preemptively during the launch window.
9. **Launch Success Metrics & Kill Criteria** — the specific, pre-committed metrics (activation, early retention, CAC-to-plan variance) that define launch success, and the explicit conditions under which the launch plan is paused or revised rather than pushed through on schedule.
10. **Post-Launch Iteration Loop** — how launch-week and launch-month learnings feed back into segment sequencing and channel mix for the weeks immediately following, before Market Expansion (Doc 20) planning begins.

## Deliverables
* Approved GTM Strategy document with a named primary launch segment and named primary channel(s).
* A launch readiness checklist mapped to the Pre-Launch Readiness Gates section, signed off by each gating function.
* A rollout wave plan with explicit graduation criteria between waves.
* A pre-committed launch success/kill-criteria scorecard.

## Dependencies

- [ ] Pre-launch readiness gates confirmed met or explicitly waived (with named accountable owner) by each gating function (Trust & Safety, Engineering, Product).
- [ ] Launch success metrics and kill criteria are quantified and pre-committed before launch spend begins, not defined retroactively.
- [ ] Rollout wave plan reviewed for graduation-criteria clarity by Product and Growth/Marketing jointly.
