# Document 22: Localization Strategy (Business Layer)

## Document Name
Localization Strategy (Business Layer)

## Purpose
Define the business and commercial decisions governing localization investment — which markets receive full localization funding, which receive a minimal baseline, and which are served with no localization beyond the product default — as the commercial counterpart to Phase 2's Localization & Regional Adaptation Experience (Doc 42). Where Doc 42 defines what localizes at the product/UX level (language, currency, cultural calendar, financial parsing conventions) and what never localizes (core philosophy, the Proactivity Ladder, trust/consent rules), this document defines which markets are funded to receive that localization work and to what depth, and why.

## Why It Exists
Phase 2's Localization & Regional Adaptation Experience answers "what must adapt for the product to feel native" — but it does not, and should not, answer "which of the many markets we could theoretically enter get that adaptation funded first, fully, or at all." Without a business-layer decision on top of it, localization spend tends to follow whichever market is loudest internally (a founder's home market, a market that recently signed a partnership) rather than the markets the Market Definition Document's sizing and the company's actual expansion sequencing say deserve it. This document exists to make localization a tiered, criteria-driven commercial investment — full localization for markets that justify the cost, a minimal baseline for markets served opportunistically, and an explicit decision to defer or decline localization elsewhere — so that Design and Product only build against Phase 2's "what localizes" playbook for markets that have actually cleared the business case to receive it.

## Approximate Page Count
6-8 pages

## Sections
1. **Localization Tiering Model** — the defined tiers (e.g., full localization, minimal/baseline localization, English-only/default) a market is assigned to, and the criteria distinguishing each tier.
2. **Investment Criteria per Tier** — the revenue potential, market size, and strategic-importance criteria (drawn from the Market Definition Document's TAM/SAM/SOM by geography) that determine which tier a given market qualifies for.
3. **Full Localization Tier Scope** — for markets receiving full investment, which of Phase 2 Doc 42's localization sections (financial/transaction parsing conventions, language and tone adaptation, cultural calendar and scheduling norms, regional food/nutrition data) are funded in full, and the expected commercial return justifying that scope.
4. **Minimal Localization Tier Scope** — for markets receiving baseline investment only, which subset of Phase 2 Doc 42's adaptation surface is funded (typically currency/date formatting and basic language support) without full financial-parsing or cultural-calendar investment.
5. **Localization Budget & Cost Allocation Model** — how localization headcount, vendor spend, and ongoing maintenance budget are allocated across tiers, and how that allocation is reviewed against actual market performance.
6. **Tier Transition Criteria** — the performance thresholds that move a market from minimal to full localization investment (or the reverse), coordinated with the Market Expansion (Phase 8, Doc 20) trigger criteria so localization investment tracks actual market traction rather than moving independently of it.
7. **Localization Debt & Deprioritization Policy** — the explicit process for reducing or sunsetting localization investment in an underperforming market, including how existing localized users are communicated with and supported through a de-investment decision.
8. **Vendor & Partnership Strategy for Localization Execution** — how translation agencies, regional financial-data partners (e.g., for SMS/UPI-format parsing conventions), and cultural-content reviewers are sourced and managed per tier.
9. **Governance & Sign-off Model** — who assigns a market to a tier, who approves tier transitions, and how the decision is recorded and revisited on a defined cadence.

## Deliverables
* Approved Localization Strategy (Business Layer) document, including the tiering model and per-tier investment criteria.
* A live-maintained market-to-tier assignment list, reviewed on a defined cadence.
* A tier transition and localization-debt deprioritization policy.
* A localization budget allocation model reusable across current and future markets.
